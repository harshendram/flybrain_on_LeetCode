What am I watching?

The fly is flybody, the anatomically detailed fruit-fly model built by Google DeepMind and HHMI Janelia (Vaxenburg et al., Nature 2025), here decimated for the browser. Its choices come from a model of its real mushroom body, wired neuron by neuron from the MaleCNS connectome, the same one you can watch learn on the brain page.

A mushroom body's job in a real fly is to decide approach or avoid for a smell it has learned about. Here each feeder is labelled with an algorithm technique, and the fly approaches the one its output neurons vote for. The decision is the model's; the flight only shows it. Its zig-zag width comes from the margin between its top two votes, echoing how real flies cast across an odour plume when they lose the scent.

A right answer earns sugar (reward dopamine, PAM); a wrong one a shock (punishment dopamine, PPL1). Both change the same synapses the brain page shows, so the fly learns as you play. Honest numbers: its top guess is right about 40% of the time on problems it never saw (always guessing the most common technique gets 31%).
